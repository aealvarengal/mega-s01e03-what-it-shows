#4471 says one thing: the parser reads dates month first, 412 reports, one fix queued. That is true of some of the reports. It is not true of all of them.

#4471 is not one issue. It is two, merged because [decision 0001](/kb/decisions/0001-dedupe-by-subject) merges on subject line alone, and both read the same to a tired desk at 9am.

The table shows two automatic causes, parser and hint — but they are one real problem, not two: nothing in this pipeline checks the tenant’s actual locale before reading or hinting a date. That single defect shows up two ways, by channel. The table’s own footnote — reports “read as a one-day move” — is the tell for the real second problem: a month/day swap moves a date by weeks or months; these reports move it by exactly one day. That is not this bug at all.

The two problems

  1. Date reading isn’t locale-aware. On app/email/booking-v2-form traffic, the shared parser reads a correctly-typed date as MM/DD regardless of locale, flipping it for day-first tenants (parser, 379 reports). On booking-v3-form traffic, the form’s hint tells those same tenants to type MM/DD/YYYY, and the form then reads what they typed the tenant’s actual day-first way — flipping it for the opposite reason (hint, 33 reports). Same defect, two symptoms. 412 reports — the full ticket, counted as the desk’s own cause split already has it. → issue #1
  2. The one-day bug. Five of those same 412 — flagged by desk-notes.json and footnoted on the table above — are off by exactly one day, not a month/day swap: a meeting that showed one date in Lisbon and another in Toronto, a month where every booking ran a day late and only that month, an invite that arrived before the booking it was for. They stay filed under parser above (that’s where causeOf() puts them), but the actual cause is a timezone or day-boundary problem, unrelated to date-order at all. 5 reports, a subset of the 412, not an addition to it. → issue #3 (issue #2 was opened and then folded into #1 once it was clear it was the same defect, not a second one — see the comment on #1.)

Why the desk didn’t see it

Decision 0001 is right that merging on subject is fast and that splitting later is cheaper than two engineers chasing the same bug. It says nothing about the other direction: two different bugs can also share a subject line, and merging them hides that there were ever two. “Merge when unsure” needs a partner rule: before you mark a big ticket known, check whether every report in it actually shares a cause, not just a subject. causes.ts already has the logic to check this; #4471 just never ran it before someone wrote the word “known” on the page.

Lessons

  • A ticket this size is not “mostly one bug reported many times” by default — it is one bug only if something actually checked.
  • A footnote on a table is a finding somebody has to read and act on, not decoration. This one sat there since the table was built.
  • The desk’s own tool for telling causes apart (causes.ts) already existed. Nobody pointed it at #4471 until now.
