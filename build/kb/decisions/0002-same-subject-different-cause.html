Date: 31 October 2026 · Deciders: Alejandro Alvarenga (harness-assisted), support desk

Context

Decision 0001 merges two reports into one ticket when their subject lines match, on the sound logic that duplicates of the same bug usually share a subject. It says nothing about the reverse case: two different bugs can also produce reports with the same subject line, because people describe symptoms (“wrong day”, “date is wrong”), not causes.

Ticket #4471 was exactly this. 412 reports, one subject-line cluster, marked “known” as a single parser bug. Run through the desk’s own cause logic (src/lib/kb/causes.ts), it splits cleanly into two: parser (379 reports) and hint (33 reports) — two different components, two different fixes, merged into one ticket by decision 0001’s own rule.

Decision

Before a big ticket is marked known, run its reports through the desk’s cause-splitting tool (causes.ts), not just eyes on the subject line. If it reports more than one cause, file one ticket per cause, each with its own count — not one ticket for the whole merge.

Filed from this review:

  • issue #1 — the parser bug, 379 reports.
  • issue #2 — the booking-v3 hint bug, 33 reports.

See the full breakdown.

Consequences

  • Decision 0001 still stands for genuine duplicates. This adds a check in the other direction: same subject is necessary for a merge, not sufficient to assume one cause.
  • A ticket this size needs its cause distribution checked once, before it’s marked known — not reopened and resplit later once someone notices the fix didn’t close all of it.
  • causes.ts already existed to do this check. Running it on every big ticket going forward is a habit, not new tooling.
